# Style specification

This document governs every chapter. It replaces the seven-slot lesson template and the
instructional register that preceded it.

## Register

Chapters are written as **review-article prose**. Concretely:

- **Third person, impersonal.** No "you", no "we" except where reporting what this work
  did ("we simulate", "the analysis presented here"). Not "you should check the overlay"
  but "alignment should be verified against the image before spatial claims are made".
- **Claims are carried by citations, not by assertion.** Any statement about what a
  platform does, what a method recovers, or how often something fails needs a reference.
  Where no reference exists, say so explicitly rather than stating it flatly.
- **Hedge where the literature is unsettled, and say why.** "Benchmarks disagree on the
  ranking of domain-detection methods, and most evaluate on a single Visium dataset
  against which several were tuned" is preferable to either endorsing a method or
  omitting the problem.
- **Quantities carry units and provenance.** Microns for length. Vendor specifications
  are attributed as such ("10x reports") and distinguished from independent measurement
  ("an independent comparison measured").
- **No rhetorical questions, no direct address, no imperatives** outside figure captions
  and code comments.
- British spelling.

What does not change from the previous material: precision, the refusal to overstate,
and the emphasis on failures that produce plausible output rather than errors. Those
survive the change of register; only the voice changes.

## Chapter structure

Each chapter is numbered and uses standard scientific sectioning. The canonical shape:

```
# N. Title

## N.1 Introduction
      What question the chapter addresses and why it is difficult. One or two paragraphs.

## N.2 Background
      What is measured, in what units, by what physical process. Cited.

## N.3 [Substantive sections, numbered N.3, N.4, ...]
      The material itself. Subsections as the content requires.

## N.x Failure analysis
      A characteristic failure of this topic, demonstrated rather than described.
      Where a simulation with known ground truth can demonstrate it, it is used and the
      discrepancy is quantified.

## N.y Open questions
      What the field has not settled, stated as open questions rather than as a ranking.

## References
      Numbered or author-date, consistent within the chapter.
```

Not every chapter needs every section, but the failure analysis is not optional. It is
the contribution that distinguishes this work from the existing literature, and it is
retained from the previous design for that reason.

## Figures

- Numbered and captioned: **Figure N.1.** Caption text as a complete sentence stating
  what is shown and what it demonstrates.
- Every figure is generated by committed code. No figure is drawn by hand or described
  without being produced.
- Palettes are validated for colour-vision deficiency by `data/palette.py`; the check is
  computed, not assessed by eye.
- Equal aspect ratio and a scale bar in microns on any spatial plot.

## Code

Code appears where it constitutes evidence — a demonstration, a measurement, a figure —
not as a tutorial exercise. It is executed, and its output is committed to the freeze
cache. Code that cannot be executed does not appear; a chapter that needs unavailable
data says so in prose instead of shipping an unrunnable block.

`tools/scan_lessons.py` checks every chunk for names used but never assigned and for
files read that nothing produces. It exits non-zero and should gate a commit.

## Citations

Every citation is verified against PubMed or the publisher record before it is written,
and carries a DOI. Preprints and non-indexed venues are labelled. Where an online
publication year differs from the issue year, the issue is cited.

`citations-verified: true` in the front matter is a claim that this was actually done.

## Length

There is no target length. A chapter is as long as its subject requires and no longer.
The previous 5,000-word ceiling on a single section is withdrawn; what replaces it is the
requirement that every paragraph either states something cited, demonstrates something
computed, or draws a consequence from one of those.
